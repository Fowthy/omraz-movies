SELFLESS - THE STORY

A chimpanzee. A mime. A fool in love.

---

THE SETUP

A cobblestone street corner in a timeless city. Sepia tones. Flickering like old film.

Our chimp wears a striped mime shirt, white face paint (badly applied, endearingly smudged), and a tiny bowler hat that keeps falling off. He has set up a small performance space: a wooden crate for a stage, a wilting flower in a tin can, a hand-painted sign that says "PERFORMANCES - FREE (tips appreciated)".

He waits. Adjusts his hat. It falls off. He catches it. Puts it back. It falls off again.

Nobody comes.

---

ACT 1: THE FIRST GLIMPSE

Then - SHE appears.

A beautiful figure walks down the street. (Human? Another chimp? It doesn't matter. She is EVERYTHING.)

Our chimp freezes. His jaw drops. His hat falls off and he doesn't even notice. Hearts practically float out of his eyes.

She walks past without looking.

He scrambles to perform - but she's already gone. He stands there, one arm extended in an unfinished gesture, mime-frozen in the most pathetic "reaching for love" pose ever attempted.

His hat finally lands on his head. Perfectly. Now that nobody's watching.

---

ACT 2: THE INVISIBLE WALL

She returns! Walking the other way!

This time he's ready. He leaps into his act: THE INVISIBLE WALL.

He mimes pressing against glass, face smooshed comically, hands sliding down the invisible surface. Classic mime stuff. He's actually pretty good.

But he's so focused on impressing her that he doesn't notice - he's miming TOWARD the actual wall. He smooshes his face against real brick. Bonk.

He staggers back, sees stars, tries to play it off as part of the act.

She walks past. Doesn't notice. Was looking at her phone. (Anachronistic? Yes. Funnier? Also yes.)

He deflates. The wilting flower in the tin can droops further, sympathetically.

---

ACT 3: THE WIND

She appears again! Third time's the charm!

He launches into an elaborate "WALKING AGAINST THE WIND" routine. He leans dramatically forward, coat flapping, hat flying off (he catches it! crowd-pleaser!), struggling heroically against imaginary gusts.

The performance is genuinely impressive. Athletic. Committed. He's giving his ALL.

She slows down. Is she... watching?

He doubles down. MORE wind! STRONGER wind! He's practically horizontal!

Then - actual wind. A real gust. It catches his sign, his flower, his hat, his dignity. Everything scatters. He pinwheels his arms, stumbles, trips over his crate-stage, and lands in a pile of his own props.

She's gone. Was she ever really watching? He'll never know.

He lies there. A pigeon lands on him. He doesn't even shoo it away.

---

ACT 4: THE BOX

Later. He's rebuilt his little stage. Determined.

This time he tries "TRAPPED IN A BOX." A classic. Foolproof.

He carefully mimes the walls. Front. Back. Sides. Top. He's trapped! He pushes! He struggles! He emotes!

She walks by.

He REALLY commits. He needs her to see. He throws himself against his invisible walls, genuinely distressed now, the comedy tipping into something uncomfortable. Is he acting anymore? Is he actually trapped?

She stops. She turns. She's LOOKING.

He freezes. Eye contact. This is it.

She reaches into her purse... pulls out a coin... drops it in his tip jar.

"Nice box," she says. And walks away.

He stands there. In his invisible box. Holding the single coin. It's not nothing. But it's not what he wanted.

He slowly mimes opening a door in his box. Steps out. The metaphor is not subtle.

---

ACT 5: THE GIVING

Final act. Sunset light. Golden hour on the cobblestones.

She walks by one last time. But this time, she's crying. Bad day. Bad life. Bad something. She sits on a bench across from his little stage, face in her hands.

Our chimp watches. His heart breaks. Not for himself this time.

He doesn't perform for her. Instead, he quietly walks over. Offers her his wilting flower from the tin can. It's pathetic. It's perfect.

She laughs through tears. Takes it.

He mimes... what? Pulling something from his chest. His heart? Too literal. No. He mimes pulling off his own face - his mime makeup - and offering it to her. His whole identity. His whole self.

She doesn't understand. How could she?

But she smiles. Really smiles. At him.

He mimes catching her smile - literally grabbing it from the air - and putting it in his chest where his heart was.

She laughs again. Stands up. Walks away.

But this time, she looks back.

---

FINAL IMAGE

Our chimp stands alone on his little crate-stage as twilight falls. His props are scattered. His makeup is smudged. His hat is somehow both on his head AND in his hand (don't question it).

He performs one final act: "THE BOW."

He bows deeply to an empty street. To no audience. To everyone. To her. To himself.

The pigeon lands on his head.

He doesn't mind.

---

THE MESSAGE:

We give ourselves to others hoping to be seen. Sometimes they see us. Sometimes they don't. Sometimes a wilting flower and a borrowed smile is enough.

Sometimes the performance is the point.

Selfless - giving without expecting return. And finding that's okay.
